Infodemic
A game about working out what's real in a feed. Built for classrooms.

No one ever taught us to read a feed.

Half of American teens say the news just arrives — on a phone, between a friend's post and an ad, never saying where it came from. Infodemic is a game that makes a room full of kids slow that feed down, check what's in it, and argue their way to one thing they can all stand behind.

What we're up against01

The feed is the news now.

And almost nobody arrives with the habits to read it.

How it plays02

Three rounds per story, about ten minutes.

Everyone's on their own phone. The game happens between them, out loud, in the room.

01

Judge on instinct

The whole feed lands in sixty seconds. Thumbs only, no time to think — that's the honest baseline, because it's how everyone reads a feed when nobody's watching.

02

Go check

Search it. Ask an AI. Look up who runs the account and who funds them. Everyone digs through the same evidence — and there's no answer key waiting at the end.

03

Agree on something

The table writes one community note together, each player holding a different job. It doesn't ship until it survives the skeptic sitting next to them.

The bet

Every player is handed a different version of the same story. So the disagreement at the table is real rather than staged — and the only way out of it is to go look things up and talk to each other. It's a phone game whose entire point is the conversation in the room.

2–4 players ~30 min session Phone, laptop or tablet Ages 11+ Free & open source
The curator console03

It's this morning's news, and it's your feed.

Infodemic isn't a set of canned scenarios that go stale by February. You build the feed from real stories pulled off a live news wire, set how long you've got, and publish the whole thing as one word you read to the room.

Pulled this morning, not written last year

One button pulls fresh headlines off a live news wire, and the game generates each story's framings and the evidence behind them. You aren't writing curriculum at ten at night.

You pick the stories. You never write the spin

What goes in the feed is your call. How each story gets framed — and which player sees which version — is generated at publish time. Nobody can accuse you of stacking the deck when you didn't build the deck.

It tells you when the feed is too easy

If nothing in your feed plays spun, there's nothing for a table to disagree about — and the console says so before you publish, not after the bell.

Build the feed

One person picks the topics. The round assembles itself — you don't approve stories one by one. Publishing gives you a single word, like OTTER, to read out to everyone else.

Get everyone in
