Infodemic
A game about working out what's real in a feed. Built for classrooms.

No one ever taught us to read a feed.

The feed doesn't stop. Infodemic is a classroom game that makes a room full of kids stop it — call one post on instinct, dig into it together, and argue their way to one thing they can all stand behind.

How it plays02

Four rounds per story, about ten minutes.

Everyone's on their own phone. The game happens between them, out loud, in the room.

01

Gut check

The whole feed lands in sixty seconds. Thumbs only, no time to think — that's the honest baseline, because it's how everyone reads a feed when nobody's watching.

02

Investigate

The team picks the story it split on and goes to work. Search it. Ask an AI. Look up who runs the account and who funds them. Everything anyone finds lands in one shared case file — and there's no answer key waiting at the end.

03

Spot the tell

One more look at the post, in silence. Everyone crosses out the part that was working on them — then the real tell is revealed, and so is who caught it.

04

Write the note

The team checks the case file together and flags anything that doesn't hold — out loud. Then everyone writes a community note from it, and the one the team rates most helpful goes on the post.

The bet

Everyone sees the same feed. Nobody reads it the same way. The first round shows the team exactly where it split — and that disagreement is real, not staged. The only way out of it is to go look things up and talk to each other. It's a phone game whose entire point is the conversation in the room.

2–4 players ~30 min session Phone, laptop or tablet Ages 11+ Free & open source
The curator console03

It's this morning's news, and it's your feed.

Infodemic isn't a set of canned scenarios that go stale by February. You build the feed from real stories pulled off a live news wire, set how long you've got, and publish the whole thing as one word you read to the room.

Pulled this morning, not written last year

One button pulls fresh headlines off a live news wire, and the game generates each story's framings and the evidence behind them. You aren't writing curriculum at ten at night.

You pick the stories. You never write the spin

What goes in the feed is your call. How each story gets framed — and what its tell is — is generated at publish time. Nobody can accuse you of stacking the deck when you didn't build the deck.

It tells you when the feed is too easy

If nothing in your feed plays spun, there's nothing for a table to disagree about — and the console says so before you publish, not after the bell.

Where it started

Infodemic began at Sora Schools, a project-based school for grades 6–12. The game is what came out of a school built on the idea that students should be doing real work, not answering questions someone already knew the answer to.

Where it's being built

It's being built during the Assembly Code fellowship, and it's free and open source — AGPL-3.0 on GitHub, for anyone who wants to run it, teach with it, or take it somewhere else.
