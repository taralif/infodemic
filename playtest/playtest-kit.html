Infodemic · Paper Playtest v2 — THE FEED · rules on one page

INFODEMIC

Your feed is 16 stories deep today. Some are true, some are bait, one isn't settled yet. Make sense of it — together.

You need

2–5 players · 60–90 min · these sheets, cards cut out · tokens in 3 colors (red = misinformation, yellow = biased-but-factual, green = verified) · 1 balance token · the SAME STORY strips · phones in Round 2 only. A big table or floor.

Setup (2 min)

  1. Shuffle the 16 feed cards and lay them out face-up in a loose scatter — no order, no categories. This is the feed: everything at once, nothing organized.
  2. Balance token on 0. Rating slips + a pen per player.

Round 1 — The scroll (10 min)

  1. Everyone scrolls: move around the feed and read the cards silently — the way you actually read a feed, alone, fast, no discussion.
  2. Each player secretly rates every card on their slip: R / Y / G. Instinct only. No phones.
  3. Reveal all slips at once; place everyone's tokens on each card.

Read the board: unanimous cards are settled-for-now (leave tokens). Mixed-color cards are CONTESTED — stack their tokens. Contested cards are the game.

Round 2 — The investigation (30–35 min)

Work the contested cards, most-split first. Phones on — start from each card's source line. To settle a card red or yellow: answer 2 of the Three Questions aloud, persuade the table, write a one-line case note. To turn a card GREEN: file a full Case Report — no report, no green.

Threading — the big move. Some of these cards are secretly the same story wearing different clothes. When you can show the table that two cards connect — the fake and its debunk, the hoax and the business behind it — slide them together and lay a SAME STORY strip between them. A discovered thread counts as evidence toward settling every card in it. (Beware: some cards only look connected. Prove it or drop it.)

Re-opening a settled-for-now card with new evidence is allowed and admired.

The Three Questions

  1. Who is behind this information?
  2. What is the evidence?
  3. What do other sources say?

The balance (5 min)

Each claim settled with a note or report → 1 toward ACCURATE. Each claim left contested or settled without evidence → 1 toward FALSE. Noise wins the unexamined. A card honestly marked "still unresolved — here's what would settle it" counts as ACCURATE: knowing you don't know yet is sensemaking too.

Debrief (15 min)

Reassemble the feed as threads: what did it look like scattered vs. connected? Then the facilitator shares what fact-checkers found — a story to compare notes with, never a score.
