# The Infodemic Curation Charter (v0.1 — Tara to edit)
*Who chooses what's in the feed, by what rules, and how to check our work. Published with every Feed Drop. This document exists because our game's second question — "who's behind this information?" — applies to us too.*

## The promise
Every feed you've ever scrolled was curated by someone who won't show you how. This one shows you everything: the selection rules, the sources, the judgment calls, and a way to challenge them. We are not neutral — nobody who chooses sixteen stories is — but we are **auditable**.

## How stories get chosen (per drop)
1. **Currency:** claims come from real, current coverage — most from the last two weeks; every card is dated.
2. **Mix quotas:** many subjects at once, like a real feed (no single-topic drops); roughly 7 false / 2 biased-but-factual / 6 verifiable / 1 genuinely unsettled — enough red to feel real, enough green that "everything's fake" loses.
3. **At least one hard card** per drop — a claim where the distortion sits on top of something true, and honest people can disagree.
4. **Community-informed:** subject emphasis follows what our players' community says it's worried about (currently: AI fakes, hate-based content, scams — from our parent survey).
5. **Portfolio balance:** misinformation examples are drawn from across the political and cultural spectrum *over time* — measured across drops, not forced into fake per-drop symmetry.
6. **Sensitivity tiers:** launch drops use low/medium-sensitivity topics. High-sensitivity (culture-war-coded) cases exist in a gated tier with its own consent step. We name this instead of hiding it.
7. **Hard exclusions:** no private individuals; no content sexualizing or endangering minors; no reproduction of source text or media (paraphrase + citation + link only); nothing we can't trace to public coverage.

## What the facilitator key is based on
Keys reflect the convergence of multiple independent sources (fact-checking organizations, wire reporting, primary documents) — never a single outlet's say-so. Cards marked **LIVE** have no key: nobody knows yet, and the honest call is "unresolved." The key is never used to grade players; it appears only as an epilogue.

## The audit trail
Every case ships open-source (CC BY-SA) in our public repository with: the full source list, the freshness dates, the vetting checklist as completed, and a changelog of every edit. Anyone — a kid, a parent, a school board — can trace any card to its evidence in about two minutes. Try it on your feed.

## Challenging a card
Think we got one wrong? **File a case report against the editor** — same form the players use: who's behind your counter-evidence, what is it, what do other sources say. Sustained challenges produce a published correction note on the card. We model corrections openly because that's what trustworthy information sources do — and because being corrected in public is a skill this game is supposed to teach.

## The autonomy roadmap (where this is going)
- **Now:** one shared drop — everyone at the table sees the same sixteen stories. *(Open design question, named honestly: is the shared board a feature — restoring the common ground that personalized feeds destroyed — or should players eventually shape their own drops? Currently we believe the shared board is the point.)*
- **Next:** players can **submit candidate claims** for future drops (with sources — submissions are themselves practice in the three questions).
- **Later:** a teen editorial board co-curates drops through the same charter, with the same published audit trail. The endgame of media literacy isn't distrusting feeds — it's being qualified to run one.
