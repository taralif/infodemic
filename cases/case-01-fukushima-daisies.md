## The story (facilitator/answer-key context — not shown to players)
In 2015, a photo of deformed daisies titled "Fukushima Nuclear Flowers" went viral on Imgur, captioned as showing "nuclear birth defects." The photo itself is authentic — but it was taken ~110 km from the plant, where radiation was near-normal, and the deformation is almost certainly fasciation, a common plant mutation seen worldwide with no link to radiation. SHEG used this image to test whether students could evaluate evidence; most couldn't articulate why it was weak. The twist that makes it great for the game: **the photo is real — it's the caption's causal claim that's false.**

| 1 | Feed | A photo of deformed daisies is captioned as showing "nuclear birth defects" from the Fukushima disaster. | **MISINFO** | Real photo, unsupported causal claim — no evidence links the deformation to radiation. COR: *What's the evidence?* The case's centerpiece. |

| 3 | Microblog | A post argues the daisy photo must be trustworthy because it hasn't been photoshopped. | **MISINFO** | Authenticity of the image ≠ truth of its caption. The single most important lesson in the case. |
| 4 | Search | Radiation readings at the town where the photo was taken were close to normal background levels. | **VERIFIED** | Snopes: photo from Nasushiobara, ~110 km away, modest readings. COR: *What's the evidence?* |
| 5 | Feed | A report describes scientific studies that found radiation-linked mutations in some Fukushima-area wildlife. | **BIASED-BUT-FACTUAL** | Real research exists (e.g., butterfly studies near the plant) — but it doesn't validate the daisy photo. Teaches: adjacent truth ≠ confirmation. |
| 6 | Feed | An aggregator headline announces "mutant daisies blooming near Fukushima," with the viral photo. | **BIASED-BUT-FACTUAL** | Flowers did grow in Japan and are deformed — but "near Fukushima" (110 km) and "mutant" (fasciation) are doing sensational work. Framing card. |
| 7 | Short-video | A video intercuts the daisies with Chernobyl footage, implying nuclear accidents always create mutation zones. | **MISINFO** | Insinuation by montage; no evidence connects the two. Teaches visual-editing-as-argument. |
| 8 | Microblog | A botanist replies that fasciation shows up in gardens everywhere and asks the poster where exactly the photo was taken. | **VERIFIED** | Models the investigative move itself (provenance question). Good "verification is a behavior" card. |

**Balance note:** 3 misinfo / 2 biased / 3 verified — mirrors case #0 so the one-case-vs-two-cases comparison isn't skewed by difficulty.

## Why this case earns its slot
It's the *inverse* of case #0: there the image is fake and the disaster real; here the image is real and the claim fake. Played together (Session B), the pair teaches that "is the photo real?" is never the last question — which is COR's whole point.
